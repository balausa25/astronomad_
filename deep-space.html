🌟 Star Birth

Carina Nebula — "Cosmic Cliffs"

NASA's James Webb Space Telescope captured this stunning infrared view of the Carina Nebula, revealing previously invisible areas of star birth. Located 7,600 light-years away, this "landscape" of gas and dust is actually the edge of a massive star-forming region. The tallest "peaks" in this image are about 7 light-years high.

Credit: NASA, ESA, CSA, STScI

🌌 Galaxy Collision

Stephan's Quintet

This visual grouping of five galaxies was captured by Webb in unprecedented detail. Four of the galaxies are actually interacting, locked in a cosmic dance that will eventually merge them into a single giant galaxy. The image contains over 150 million pixels and is constructed from about 1,000 separate image files.

Credit: NASA, ESA, CSA, STScI

🔭 Distant Universe

Webb's First Deep Field

The deepest and sharpest infrared image of the distant universe ever taken. This slice of space — about the size of a grain of sand held at arm's length — contains thousands of galaxies, some of which formed just 300 million years after the Big Bang. The light from the farthest galaxies took over 13 billion years to reach us.

Credit: NASA, ESA, CSA, STScI

💫 Stellar Death

Southern Ring Nebula

A planetary nebula 2,500 light-years away, captured by Webb's NIRCam and MIRI instruments. It shows a dying star shedding its outer layers, creating intricate rings of gas and dust. The dimmer star at the center has been sending out these rings for thousands of years. This nebula offers a glimpse of what our Sun might look like in its final stages.

Credit: NASA, ESA, CSA, STScI

Eyes on the Universe

🔭

James Webb Space Telescope

The most powerful space telescope ever built. It observes the universe in infrared light, revealing the first galaxies and studying exoplanet atmospheres.

🌌

Hubble Space Telescope

Launched in 1990, Hubble has made over 1.5 million observations, helped determine the age of the universe, and captured iconic images like the "Pillars of Creation".

Mind-Blowing Fact

"If you could travel at the speed of light (300,000 km/s), it would still take you 100,000 years to cross the Milky Way galaxy from end to end. And the Milky Way is just one of over 2 trillion galaxies in the observable universe."
