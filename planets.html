Visual Overview

A quick look at the inner and outer planets of our Solar System.

☿ Mercury

The smallest and fastest planet. It has no atmosphere to trap heat, causing extreme temperature swings.

♀ Venus

Earth's twin in size, but with a toxic atmosphere and surface hot enough to melt lead.

🌍 Earth

The only known planet with life. A perfect balance of water, atmosphere, and magnetic protection.

♂ Mars

The Red Planet. Home to the tallest volcano and the deepest canyon in the Solar System.

Detailed Facts

Click on each planet's name to expand and read more.

Mercury is the smallest planet in our Solar System and the closest to the Sun. It has no atmosphere, so temperatures swing from −180°C at night to 430°C during the day.

  • Day Length: 59 Earth days
  • Year Length: 88 Earth days
  • Moons: 0
  • NASA Mission: MESSENGER (2004–2015) mapped its entire surface.

Venus is the second planet from the Sun and the hottest planet in our Solar System (about 465°C). It has a runaway greenhouse effect caused by a thick atmosphere of carbon dioxide and sulfuric acid clouds.

  • Day Length: 243 Earth days (retrograde)
  • Year Length: 225 Earth days
  • Moons: 0
  • NASA Mission: Magellan mapped the surface using radar in the 1990s.

Earth is the third planet from the Sun and the only astronomical object known to harbor life. About 71% of Earth's surface is covered with water.

  • Day Length: 24 hours
  • Year Length: 365.25 days
  • Moons: 1 (The Moon)
  • NASA Mission: Landsat and Earth Observing System monitor climate and weather.

Mars is the fourth planet from the Sun and the second-smallest planet. It is a dusty, cold, desert world with a very thin atmosphere. It's known as the "Red Planet" due to iron oxide on its surface.

  • Day Length: 24.6 hours
  • Year Length: 687 Earth days
  • Moons: 2 (Phobos and Deimos)
  • NASA Mission: Perseverance rover is currently exploring Jezero Crater.

Jupiter is the largest planet in our Solar System. It is a gas giant with a mass more than two and a half times that of all the other planets combined. Its Great Red Spot is a giant storm larger than Earth.

  • Day Length: 9.9 hours
  • Year Length: 11.8 Earth years
  • Moons: 95 (including Europa, Ganymede, Io, and Callisto)
  • NASA Mission: Juno is currently orbiting and studying the planet.

Saturn is the sixth planet from the Sun and the second-largest in the Solar System. It is a gas giant with an average radius of about nine and a half times that of Earth. It has the most spectacular ring system.

  • Day Length: 10.7 hours
  • Year Length: 29.4 Earth years
  • Moons: 146 (including Titan and Enceladus)
  • NASA Mission: Cassini spent 13 years studying Saturn and its moons.

Uranus is the seventh planet from the Sun. It has the third-largest planetary radius and fourth-largest planetary mass in the Solar System. It is an ice giant, and it rotates on its side.

  • Day Length: 17.2 hours
  • Year Length: 84 Earth years
  • Moons: 27 (including Titania and Oberon)
  • NASA Mission: Voyager 2 is the only spacecraft to visit Uranus (1986).

Neptune is the eighth and farthest known planet from the Sun. In the Solar System, it is the fourth-largest planet by diameter, the third-most-massive planet, and the densest giant planet.

  • Day Length: 16.1 hours
  • Year Length: 165 Earth years
  • Moons: 14 (including Triton)
  • NASA Mission: Voyager 2 captured the first close-up images in 1989.
